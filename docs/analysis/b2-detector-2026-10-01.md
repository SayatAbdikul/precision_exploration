- **Cause of the v1 loss.** Two things, both measured one at a time and cumulatively. (1) The head: v1 stores the
  decoded, stride-scaled pixel boxes as INT8 codes (a step of 5.0 to 5.4 px, three times in a row) and re-stores
  boxes and scores in the 84-channel joins. Not storing the decoded boxes alone is worth +3.48 [+3.15, +3.94] at
  1k; leaving the whole head after the two network outputs unquantized is worth +4.06 [+3.72, +4.55]. (2) Max-abs
  activation ranges: the MSE search is worth +2.28 [+1.77, +3.20] alone and +1.98 [+1.52, +2.88] on top of the
  head change. Quantizing once after SiLU adds +0.53 [+0.19, +0.84]. Pass-through, unsigned codes and weight MSE
  are each within about 0.3 point of zero.
- **Frozen recipe** (section 5): the classifier default (unsigned at non-negative boundaries, activation MSE,
  weight MSE, max-pool pass-through) with four differences: no bias correction; SiLU fused (one quantization after
  the activation); slice and upsample pass-through as well; decoded boxes and the 84-channel joins are not k-bit
  stores. The two network outputs, the box expectation, its projection constant and the sigmoid scores remain
  k-bit codes.
- **Protocol deviation** (section 5.3): by the written freeze rule the box logits should not have been quantized
  (their exemption met the test on dev128 before the freeze) and the joins should have stayed quantized (no test
  admitted their exemption). `conformant` is `default` with those two switches set as the rule prescribes. It is
  +0.13 [-0.27, +0.57] over `default` on dev128 and +0.33 [+0.12, +0.46] on screen1k; for every sentinel format it
  is +0.11 to +0.98 over `default` (for the two block formats the nearest available recipe, which does not store
  the joins). **Which of the two recipes the paper uses is an open owner decision**; this
  document reports both.
- **Exit test** (section 6). `default`: INT8 is 1.02 [0.78, 1.52] points below FP32; the point estimate misses

## 3. Diagnosis of the v1 INT8 loss

### 3.1 Where v1 loses (scales of the sealed v1 INT8 configuration `ddc63f6e...`)

- Decoded boxes are stored as INT8 codes in pixels with a step of 5.0 px (level 0) to 5.4 px (level 2 and the
  final join), three times in a row (decode, level join, final join).
- Sigmoid scores are stored with a step of 0.0077, so every score below 0.0039 becomes 0 and is removed by the
  0.001 confidence threshold: 95.7 detections per image instead of 177.7, and 40 distinct score values in 95,713
  detections.
- The projection constant 0..15 of the distribution-focal-loss expectation is rounded to multiples of 15/127.
- Max-abs activation ranges are inflated by outliers (first SiLU: maximum 65.5, step 0.52).

## 4. Bias correction harms the detector

Empirical bias correction is part of the classifier default, but its record in Experiment B2
(`docs/analysis/b2-baseline-repair-2026-10-01.md`, sections 3 and 8) is mixed: alone on v1 max-abs it lifts
MobileNetV3 from 4.1 to 47.7 on dev512 and lowers ResNet18 by 2.54 [0.59, 4.49]; around the full INT8 recipe no
leave-one-out interval excludes zero (B2 calls it "not supported at INT8"); at INT6 it is worth +3.3 (MobileNetV2)
and +17.7 (MobileNetV3) and at FP6 +23.5 (MobileNetV3); it is harmful at INT4 (ResNet18 28.2 points) and for Posit8
on MobileNetV3 (1.9 points). On the detector it is harmful wherever it was tried:

| where | dev128 | screen1k |
|---|---|---|
| alone on v1 max-abs (`d_bias_correction`) | 32.90 -> 0.03 | 31.59 -> 0.00 |
| ladder step cum5 -> cum6 | -11.20 [-13.90, -7.53] | -10.00 [-11.11, -8.96] |
| put back into the frozen recipe (`default_bias_correction`) | not run | -14.77 [-16.15, -13.70] (38.02 -> 23.26) |

It is not an implementation error: after correction the per-channel mean residual on the 64 correction images is
about 1e-7 at every layer, and the unit test checks the correction against channel means on a small graph.
Mechanism (measured with a scratch script on dev images, not sealed evidence): the corrections are 1 to 10
percent of the channel mean up to the SPPF block and 20 to 90 percent after it; even with MSE ranges the internal
relative error of INT8 activations is 15 to 30 percent (40 percent under max-abs), and that error is
clipping-driven and spatially sparse. A per-channel mean correction moves a sparse error onto every position of a
dense prediction map; after correction the stride-32 branch gets worse (box-logit relative error at level 2 from
0.22 to 0.40). A classifier global-pools before its logits, so only the channel mean matters there; a detector
reads every position.

### 5.2 Hardware-visible semantics of the frozen recipe and of each head exception

- **Requantization points**: a convolution feeding only SiLU keeps the wide accumulator; SiLU is evaluated on the
  wide value and requantized once. This needs a wide-precision nonlinearity (or a large table) instead of v1's
  k-bit code-to-code SiLU table; it removes one requantizer per conv-SiLU pair.
- **Pass-through**: channel slice, max-pool and nearest upsample forward the codes and the scale of their source
  (no requantizer); a concatenation requantizes its inputs to one output scale (no per-group scales).
- **Signedness**: the input image, the sigmoid output and the box expectation are unsigned integer codes; the first
  convolution has an unsigned k-bit activation operand (signed-by-unsigned multiplier); all other operands signed.
- **Scales**: one FP32 scale per quantizing node (MSE), one FP32 weight scale per output channel (MSE); folded FP32
  biases unchanged (no correction).
- **Head exception 1, decoded boxes not stored (`q_boxes=false`)**: the accelerator emits the box expectation (4
  distances per anchor, in bins) as unsigned k-bit codes; anchor decode (distance to xyxy, then xywh) and stride
  scaling run in the wide FP32 postprocessor on the dequantized expectation. There is no k-bit store of pixel
  coordinates, so no 5-px quantization step; the postprocessor needs one FP32 scale per expectation tensor and a
  multiply-add per coordinate.
- **Head exception 2, no 84-channel join (`q_joins=false`)**: there is no 84-channel k-bit tensor; the postprocessor
  reads the box tensor and the score tensor separately, each with its own scale, and NMS ranks the stored score
  codes directly.
- **Unchanged from v1 in the head**: the 64-channel box-distribution logits and the 80-channel class logits leave
  the convolutions as k-bit codes; the softmax over the 16 bins and the expectation (projection constant 0..15 held
  as weight codes with one scale) run on the accelerator and are requantized to k-bit codes; the sigmoid output is
  requantized to unsigned k-bit codes. Because NMS compares score codes, equal scores are frequent (99 percent of
  scored detections share their score with another detection at INT8) and the tie rule is part of the contract
  (section 7).
- **`conformant` differs in two places.** (1) Box logits not stored: the last box convolution's accumulator is
  not requantized; the softmax over the 16 bins and the expectation take the wide 64-channel values (a wide-input
  softmax, the same kind of cost as the fused SiLU boundary) and requantize the expectation to unsigned k-bit codes.
  (2) Joins stored: the score codes leave the sigmoid as unsigned k-bit codes and are requantized at each per-level
  join and at the final join to **signed** k-bit codes with their own scales (the engine never classes a join as
  non-negative, and the protocol's unsigned list does not name the joins), so NMS compares codes with half the
  levels of the sigmoid codebook: at INT8 on screen1k 54 distinct scores and 102,265 detections, against 88 and
  152,257 under `default`. The box group of the joins is not stored (decoded boxes stay exempt).

### 5.3 Protocol deviation in the freeze, and the conformant recipe (open for the owner)

The written rule (protocol v1 `switches.head` and `freeze_rule` item 4; tests on the base cum5 by addendum 1)
was not followed in two places. Earlier versions of this document called these "post-hoc readings" and said the
box-logit result was found after the freeze; that was wrong.

1. **Box logits.** On dev128, keeping the box logits wide (`cum5_fp32_box_logits`) is +0.56 [+0.21, +0.87] over
   cum5: the test is met. Its bootstrap vector was written at 19:57:51 and `frozen.py` at 20:04:01, so the
   interval existed when the recipe was frozen, and the `frozen.py` docstring quotes other rows of the same dev128
   table. The first agent had classed both logit exemptions as diagnostics at 19:48, before running them, and kept
   the box logits as k-bit codes. Size of the choice around `default`: +0.31 [-0.03, +0.73] on dev128, +0.43
   [+0.30, +0.60] at 1k (`default_fp32_box_logits` 38.46).
2. **Joins.** The join exemption was adopted together with the box exemption and no arm tested it before the
   freeze; the rule keeps an untested tensor quantized. Measured afterwards (`default_q_joins`), putting the score
   group of the joins back costs 0.19 [0.08, 0.43] on dev128 and 0.08 [0.06, 0.22] at 1k, so it would not have met
   the test either. This put-back stores the scores with signed codes (see 5.2), so it measures a re-store that
   also loses the sign bit; a re-store under the unsigned rule was not measured. The argument for the exemption
   (once the boxes are not a k-bit store, the 84-channel join is an artefact of the exported graph, not a hardware

## 7. Ties among quantized scores

**Rule used by the v1 evaluation.** Ultralytics `non_max_suppression`, then COCOeval: candidates enter torchvision
NMS in anchor-major, class-minor order and NMS sorts scores on the GPU, whose order among equal scores is not
specified; COCOeval then orders equal scores by a stable sort, that is by emitted order inside an image and by
evaluation image order across images.

**Fixed rule (every new measurement, FP32 included).** A total order, score descending, then anchor index
ascending, then class index ascending, for NMS, for the 300-detection cut and for emission; evaluation image order
= ascending image sha256 (the frozen panel order); COCOeval's stable sort then completes the order. On 128 screen
images the fixed rule reproduces the sealed v1 detections exactly for all five regression configurations, so on
that sample the v1 order among equal scores was already this one, and the v1 numbers do not change under it.

**Sensitivity (screen1k, both recipes; `ties-1000-r3.{json,csv}`, addendum 4).** Seven orders: the fixed rule;
the tie key reversed with the image order reversed; four random tie keys, each with a random image order; and
`imageid`, the fixed within-image rule with the images in ascending COCO image_id, which is the order plain
pycocotools uses when the original image ids are kept. The first version of this section had only the first six,
for `default`. Envelope: all orders of equal scores across detections at fixed image-local matching, true
positives first (upper bound) and false positives first (lower bound); the envelope is an oracle that no order
reaches, and it only bounds what the tie order can do.

unchanged except that LOG8 and BFP6 swap (34.35 and 34.22 under `conformant`, an unpaired 0.13). At 6 bits the
signed score re-store in the joins removes even more low-score detections (INT6 30,634, FP6 54,715) than
`default` does, without lowering mAP.

- Under the repaired recipe Posit8 is level with INT8 (+0.09 [-0.32, +0.53]); under v1 it was at 0. This matches the
  classifier finding of B2.
- The v1 percentile values of the scalar formats are tie-dominated (section 7) and the v1 max-abs values carry the
  v1 head; the "minus v1" columns therefore measure the repair, not the formats.
- The head decision was taken on INT8 evidence only. For every other format, leaving the projection, expectation
  and scores wide gains 0.3 to 1.6 points with intervals excluding zero. At 6 bits the score store has a coarse step
  and drops most low-score detections below the 0.001 threshold (INT6 63,961 detections against 172,218 with wide
  scores; FP6 56,480 against 189,453). The frozen recipe stays the headline; a per-format head policy would need its
  own protocol.
- bfp6 and mxfp8_e4m3 use the block semantics of `tools/experiment_b_ext/shared.py` (blocks of 32 along channels
  for stored activations and along the reduction axis for weights and patches, block max-abs covering power-of-two
  scale). In v1 the range rule only selects each block's scale from its 32 values, where the 99.9th percentile is
  close to the maximum, so their v1 max-abs and percentile results nearly coincide (BFP6 11.96 and 12.02, MXFP8
  19.11 and 19.11 with 43 more detections); the new engine uses block max-abs only.

## 11. Limitations and open questions

- dev128 is a subset of screen1k; screen1k values are not an independent confirmation of decisions taken on
  dev128, and the whole study is development evidence on screen lists chosen before it.
- **Protocol deviation, owner decision (5.3).** The frozen `default` departs from the written freeze rule at the
  box logits (exemption met the test before the freeze) and at the joins (exempted without a test). The recipe the
  rule prescribes, `conformant`, was measured under addendum 3: INT8 38.35, -0.69 [-1.22, -0.53] from FP32 and
  +0.33 [+0.12, +0.46] over `default`, and at least as good as `default` for every sentinel format (block formats:
  nearest available recipe, joins not stored). The owner
  decides which recipe the paper uses; the alternative of a hardware-motivated join exemption (box logits wide,
  joins not stored: `default_fp32_box_logits`, INT8 38.46) is neither recipe and was never subjected to the rule.
- `conformant` stores the joins with signed codes (the engine's definition of a join); a re-store under the
  unsigned rule was not measured.
- Fused SiLU was admitted on dev128 (+1.42) but its gain at 1k around the frozen recipe is +0.43; it is also the one
  difference with a real hardware cost (a wide-precision SiLU).
- The head decision was made at INT8; formats with coarser score steps would gain 0.3 to 1.6 points from wide
  head stores (section 8).
- The bias-correction mechanism in section 4 rests on a scratch measurement that was not sealed.
- The seconds per image of these runs are not reported (no run was exclusive on the GPU).
- Tie order (section 7): seven orders move INT8 by up to 0.16 and the 6-bit formats by up to 0.20; this is larger
  than the INT6 recipe difference (+0.11) and than the 0.02 by which INT8 `default` misses the one-point target.
  Every reported number uses the fixed rule (images in sha256 order); plain pycocotools (image_id order) gives INT6
  about 0.19 higher under both recipes, every other configuration within 0.07.
- The figure (`results/figures/b2-detector-v1.*`) shows `default` only; the `conformant` values are in the tables
  of sections 5.3 and 8 and in `conformant-1000-r3`.
