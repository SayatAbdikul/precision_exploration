## The story in five sentences

1. Sub-8-bit non-integer formats (minifloats, posits) are usually evaluated by FP32 fake quantisation with a
   wide FP32 accumulator, and their accumulator is then sized either at FP32 or by a closed-form worst-case
   width, so the accumulator's share of MAC cost is set without measuring what accuracy needs.
2. We execute PTQ networks exactly in the integer code domain, which lets us certify a per-layer lossless
   accumulator width from the trained weight codes and the admitted code range, extending A2Q's integer
   weight-norm bound to minifloat and posit grids; it lies 2 bits below the exact data-type bound [A3].
3. Sweeping a saturating accumulator below the certificate on ResNet18, accuracy stays within 1 pp of exact down
   to 6 to 8 bits below the certificate for integer and minifloat formats (18 for posit8) and then collapses
   within 2 to 3 bits (MobileNets, INTERIM: 4 to 6 bits below, at the same absolute width for INT8 and FP8 E4M3),
   while binary16 and 21-bit float accumulators with a placed binary point and FP32 stay within 1 pp, so no float
   failure width is measured yet [A1, A2, A4, A5, A6].
4. Joined to synthesised integer MAC area, narrowing INT8 from the certified to the measured 1-pp width saves
   about a fifth to a quarter of the MAC core (27.3 % in the study's flow, 19.7 % with `-noalumacc`), provided the
   register matches the engine's (bias outside, unsigned activation port, engine tap order) [A7, C2] (today:
   integer MACs only, no power; see "Status of the headline" below).
5. Exact execution also shows that, on ResNet18 at 1k images, the FP32 simulator's apparent accuracy gap is a
   tie-breaking artefact of quantised logits (common-rule gap +0.1 to +0.2 pp, intervals up to ±1.1 pp), so there
   the simulator is usable for ranking once a tie rule is fixed (MobileNets at 1k: INTERIM, scatter without a
   consistent bias), but not for accumulator decisions, which need the exact code domain [B1, B2].

## Contributions (worded inside the audit's verdicts and its three conditions)

Audit conditions (L1201-1209): (1) scope to sub-8-bit non-integer PTQ formats with a measured failure width, and
compare with [Nat25], [Blu24], [Des23], [Ber22], [Lut24] at 8 bits; (2) show the certificate is tighter than the
[Agg24]/[Ugu17] formula for a reason other than convention; (3) make the headline the measured failure point joined
to ASIC area and energy.

1. **A measured accumulator failure width for sub-8-bit non-integer PTQ formats on edge CNNs, against a certified
   lossless reference.** Per format: certified width, event-free width, narrowest width within 1 pp of exact, and
   collapse width, for a saturating fixed-point register; binary16 and 21-bit float accumulators are reported as
   non-inferiority results only, since neither has a failure point (ledger A5, `gaps.md` G3) (conditions 1 and 3).
   Integer
   formats are included as anchors only: accumulator reduction with guarantees for integers is published
   ([Col23], [Col24a], [Col24b], [Umu25]).
2. **A per-layer, weight-aware certificate for non-integer code domains, used as the lossless reference of the
   sweep** (condition 2). Printed with three columns per format (formula as printed, exact data-type bound,
   certificate) and only the 2 substance bits claimed; the rest of the gap to the published formula is form (1 bit)
   and format convention (0 to 2 bits) (`docs/analysis/closest-prior-work-accumulators-2026-10-02.md` L238-262).
   Worded as "the minifloat and posit transcription of A2Q's per-channel weight-norm bound ([Col23] Sec. 3.2; also
   [Umu25]) on the Kulisch grid ([Ugu17])", not as a new kind of bound; it is a method contribution that makes
   contribution 1 measurable rather than a result on its own.
3. **A cost join at equal clock** in one open 55 nm flow: MAC core area at the certified, event-free and measured
   1-pp widths. (Not "complete cost changes the ranking": that direction is published in [Agg24], [Dam24], [Des23],
   [vB23], [Col24b]; audit L996-998.) Today it is integer-only and conditional: the RTL register is not the engine's
   (bias inside, signed operand port), and the area per accumulator bit depends on a synthesis option (lane P3's
   review, `docs/analysis/ics55-integer-accwidth-review-2026-10-02.md` L25-34). Against [Nat25], [Lut24], [Des23]
   and [Ber22], which have FP8 ASIC datapaths, this is the weak half (P1 L34-35).
4. **Methods: exact code-domain execution as the reference for low-bit simulators.** The simulator-versus-exact gap
   is attributed (tie rule, accumulation, scale and store rounding) and is within the 1k resolution under a common
   tie rule. Worded as "the non-integer, sub-8-bit, attributed version of a known concern" ([LiM21]; audit
   L1068-1071), not as the first observation that simulation and deployment differ.

Supporting, not contributions: recipe dependence of the format order (C1, partially covered: [Kuz22], [ZhaM26],
[LiM21]); accumulator-aware PTQ at equal width (D4) as the baseline the audit demands (L1122-1126).

## Title options

1. Accumulator Width, Not Multiplier Width: Certified and Measured Accumulators for Sub-8-Bit CNN Inference
2. How Narrow Can the Accumulator Be? Exact Code-Domain Evaluation of Sub-8-Bit Formats for Edge CNNs
3. Hardware-Cost-Aware Exploration of Sub-8-Bit Numerical Formats for Edge CNN Inference (the plan's title; broad,
   and promises more breadth of hardware than the paper will have)
4. Certified Lossless and Measured Failing Accumulator Widths for Sub-8-Bit Minifloat and Posit PTQ

Option 1 is not supported today and should not be used unless new hardware data bear it out: the ratio of an
accumulator bit to a multiplier step ("2.3 to 3.0" accumulator bits per multiplier step, accwidth L7-8) holds at a 32-bit accumulator only (at
ACC 64 the multiplier slope is 314.1 units, `docs/analysis/ics55-integer-accwidth-2026-10-01.md` L155; lane P3
finds 1.4 to 1.8 accumulator bits per multiplier step at ACC 16 and 3.8 to 4.8 at ACC 64, review L34), it depends
on a synthesis option (review L15, L33), and it exists for integer MACs only. Options 2 and 4 make no cost claim
and are the safe choices today.

## Fallback framing (audit L1209-1211)

The audit says: "If the sweep finds no format-dependent failure width, option (c) (the C4 decomposition) is the
stronger remaining result and MLSys or a journal is the better venue."

**What the data show today (1k development, one calibration draw; ledger A1, A2, A6):**

- **ResNet18 (reviewed).** In absolute bits the failure width is format dependent: W_acc(1.0) is 15 (INT6), 17
  (FP6 E2M3), 18 (INT8 signed), 19 (INT8), 23 (FP7 E3M3), 32 (posit8), 39 (FP8 E4M3), and not reachable at ≤ 63 bits
  for FP8 E5M2 (sweep L36-43). Relative to the certificate it is nearly format independent: certificate −
  W_acc(1.0) is 8, 8, 8, 6, 8, 8 bits for INT8, INT8 signed, INT6, FP6 E2M3, FP7 E3M3, FP8 E4M3, and 18 for posit8.
- **MobileNetV2 / V3-Large (INTERIM, unreviewed, 8 of 10 cases; Q1 L15-30).** W_acc(1.0) INT8 19 / 19, FP6 E2M3
  15 / 17, FP7 E3M3 23 / –, FP8 E4M3 39 / 39: for INT8, FP7 and FP8 E4M3 the absolute width equals ResNet18's,
  while the certificates are 2 to 3 bits lower (INT8 25 / 24 against 27; FP8 E4M3 45 against 47), so the slack is
  4-6 bits instead of 6-8.
- **Reading.** Absolute widths are counted on each format's product grid, so a 2-bit difference between formats
  follows from the grid alone (protocol draft 2, `docs/analysis/confirmation-protocol-draft2-2026-10-02.md`
  L148-151); H3 in its draft-1 form ("k* differs by at least 2 bits") is therefore uninformative. In the relative
  form the slack is near constant across formats within a network (posit8 excepted) but, if Q1 holds, not
  across networks. The INTERIM MobileNet data suggest a third reading that no document has tested: the absolute
  failure width per format is nearly network-independent (INT8 19 on all three networks, FP8 E4M3 39), which would
  make it a property of the format's code grid rather than of the network's weights. None of these readings is
  confirmed; the audit's risk paragraph (L1213-1216: "every format tolerates narrow accumulators down to a common
  width ... still a design rule, but a weaker one") remains the honest default.
- **Before choosing the framing:** (1) Q1's write-once summary and a review; (2) an explanation of the posit8
  exception; (3) the owner's choice of the H3 claim form ([OC-6c] in protocol draft 2, L145-151: the draft-1 form or
  the slack form "A is shown non-inferior at least 2 bits further below its certificate than a width at which B is
  shown inferior").

**Fallback if the format dependence reduces to the grid and the cost join stays integer-only:** lead with
contribution 4 (exact code-domain execution, the attributed simulator gap, the tie-rule effect and FP32-accumulator
equivalence) plus the certificate (contribution 2) and the certificate-minus-slack rule as a supporting design rule.
The audit names MLSys 2027 (deadline 2026-10-30, too close) or a journal (IEEE TCAD) as the better fit for that
version. P1 found no paper that measures network-level simulator fidelity (P1 L36, L299-301), so C4 is unchanged.
